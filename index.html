Model 2 · The dangerous moment

Plaque rupture — step by step

Most heart attacks don't come from the biggest plaque — they come from a soft one whose thin cap tears. Press play, or step through it one moment at a time.

Drag to rotate · scroll to zoom

Step 1 — A quiet plaque. Under a smooth cap, a soft pool of fat (the lipid core) sits inside the artery wall. The channel still looks almost normal — which is why this kind of plaque gives no warning.

Model 3 · The factory

3D liver — where cholesterol is made

Your liver makes most of your cholesterol at one key step: an enzyme called HMG-CoA reductase. That exact step is where atorvastatin acts. Yellow dots are fresh cholesterol being packed into VLDL particles and shipped out.

Drag to rotate · scroll to zoom

No statin. HMG-CoA reductase (the glowing marker) runs at full speed, the liver makes cholesterol freely, and VLDL particles leave loaded — the start of the chain that ends with LDL 130.

Model 4 · The journey

Cholesterol pathway — liver to artery wall, and back

Follow the particles. VLDL leaves the liver loaded with fat, shrinks to IDL, then LDL — and some LDL slips into the artery wall. HDL runs the return trip, carrying cholesterol back to the liver, where it leaves the body as bile. Built with an example patient's numbers.

Drag to rotate · scroll to zoom
LDL 130 mg/dL HDL 50 mg/dL Triglycerides 150 mg/dL VLDL ≈ 30 mg/dL Total 212 mg/dL
  • VLDL (leaves the liver, fat-loaded)
  • IDL (half-emptied)
  • LDL (cholesterol-rich)
  • HDL (return trip)
  • Bile (way out)

Why the numbers matter: with LDL at 130, far more LDL particles are circulating than HDL can tidy up — so more of them end their journey inside the artery wall. The statin's job is to cut the supply at the liver (Model 3) so fewer particles ever set off.
